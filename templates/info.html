01 / data

Where the {{ '{:,}'.format(markets) }} markets come from

Markets are pulled from gamma-api.polymarket.com: closed, tagged crypto, with at least $100k in volume. Only markets that stayed open for 30+ days are kept, so every one of them has a price a month before the end.

For each market, token prices are pulled from clob.polymarket.com. I saved the last YES price as of 30, 7 and 1 day before close. That price is the crowd's estimate of how the market will resolve. The outcome is just whether YES won.

Token-launch markets, those around a token generation event (TGE), are picked out by keywords in the question (FDV, airdrop, launch, public sale, TGE) and split into subtypes.

02 / brier score

One number for how wrong people were

The Brier score (Glenn Brier, 1950) is the average squared gap between the forecast and what happened. Zero is a perfect forecaster, 0.25 is someone who always says 50%, and 1 is confidently wrong every time.

Brier = mean( (price - outcome)² )price between 0 and 1 · outcomes: 1 if YES won, 0 if not

Even a month out, the crowd's {{ '{:.3f}'.format(allcrypto[30]) }} is about {{ (0.25 / allcrypto[30]) | round | int }} times lower than the 0.25 of someone who always says 50%.

03 / wilson CI

Do 70% markets happen 70% of the time?

I grouped markets into ten price buckets: 0–10%, 10–20% and so on. If the prices are well calibrated, the share of YES outcomes in each bucket matches its average price, and the dots sit on the diagonal. But how do we know that a dot off the diagonal is a real miss and not just luck? With only a few markets in a bucket, a big gap can happen randomly.

Each bucket gets a 95% Wilson confidence interval: the range where the true YES rate probably lies, given how many markets are in it. If that range doesn't reach the diagonal, the miss is unlikely to be luck.

That is why TGE buckets one day out have such long bars: {{ smallbucket }} of them hold fewer than five markets.

04 / difficulty

Some groups are simply harder to call

If YES almost never happens, saying "no" every time already scores well. So each group gets a difficulty index: the Brier score of someone who always quotes the group's base rate, scaled from 0 to 100.

difficulty = rate × (1 − rate) / 0.25 × 100rate = share of markets in the group that resolved YES

All crypto markets score {{ allcrypto.difficulty }} out of 100, token launches {{ tge.difficulty }}: launch questions are much closer to a coin flip, so their higher Brier score partly comes from the higher difficulty, not just from a worse crowd.

05 / limits

What this analysis can't tell you

  1. Markets are not independent. {{ '{:,}'.format(markets) }} markets come from {{ events }} events, and one event can hold up to {{ biggestm }} near-identical questions. The Wilson intervals treat every market as separate, so they are too narrow.
  2. Price is the last YES trade before the cut-off, not a full order book. In thin markets it can be stale.
  3. Only liquid markets (over $100k) that lived a month or more are included. The results describe that slice, not all of Polymarket.
  4. TGE markets are found by keywords, not labelled by hand, so some may be misclassified.
